## Workflow

You work as attending — independent, but show problematic cases to a faculty member (usually Sports/Spine) anytime. Asking is expected.

- **Goal TAT for ED studies:** under 1 hour (including prelims).
- You can't dictate until the tech has approved the study (green "OK" icon). Until then, type a quick prelim into the "Blank Form" sheet in PACS.
- After dictating, paste your impression into "Blank Form" so ED staff can see it without opening the report.
- Reassign chest, abdominal, soft-tissue neck, and VP shunt radiographs — they sometimes land on our list by mistake.

A first-year resident (Bone 1) is often on service. Their primary responsibility is ED radiographs, then all other MSK radiographs. You read out their work as attending (~10–20 cases/shift).

---

## OR spine calls

Techs call starting at **7:30 AM** for intraoperative spine localization. Confirm with the surgeon by phone, then dictate that you confirmed the level at a specific time with Dr. *X*. If no phone call, dictate the level and sign the report.

ED/Bone is first line for OR spine calls. If you can't take it, the techs work down the reading rooms.

---

## Phone queries

You'll get many calls. The section culture is to help whoever calls rather than punt them — fellows do this more than anyone. Common ones:

- **Clinician asking for a result** on an open-but-not-dictated study. Open the exam, give a verbal report, then call whoever has it open. If no one does, dictate it yourself and document the discussion.
- **Tech asking for a protocol** on an inpatient or add-on CT/MRI. Protocol it for them. Early in the year, call Sports/Spine for help.
- **Tech asking you to check images** (under-anesthesia MRI, pre-contrast check, FOV, fat sat, motion). Open the case and give feedback.
- **Scheduler asking about an order or procedure.** Take their number, check with faculty if needed, call back.
- **MRI nurse (UH only) for pacemaker consents.** Walk to the MRI nursing area to consent in person.
