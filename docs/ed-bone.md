# ED/Bone

!!! tldr "The Basics"
    - **Arrive:** by **7:15 AM**
    - **Shifts:** AM (7:15–12:30), PM (12:30–4:30), or full day (7:15–4:30). Extra stipend for covering this rotation.
    - **First thing:** over-read overnight ED radiographs (red exclamation = prelim'd by resident). Call the ED with any [discrepancies](discrepancies.md).
    - **Primary role:** ED radiographs first, then the rest of the list. Teach the Bone 1 resident. Take phone calls, OR spine reads, WebEx queries.
    - **Secondary role:** dictate all radiographs

## Workflow

You will be working as an attending, final-signing reports and teaching the Bone 1 residents — but ask for help when needed.

- **Main priority:** dictate ED radiographs (either you or the Bone 1) with TAT under 1 hour
- After that, dictate all radiographs oldest to newest
- ED/Bone is first line for responding to tech inquiries on Teams
- Radiographs we don't read (chest x-ray, abdominal x-ray, neck soft tissues, orbits, skull, dental/nasal/maxillofacial) — reassign to the correct list

---

## OR spine calls

Techs call starting at **7:30 AM** for intraoperative spine localization. Confirm with the surgeon by phone, then dictate that you confirmed the level at a specific time with Dr. *X*. If no phone call, don't dictate the study — ask the techs to move it to the Do Not Read list.

ED/Bone is first line for OR spine calls. If you can't take it, the techs work down the reading rooms.

---

## Tips

- Learn speed and efficiency — this will help you your whole career. Goal of **70–100 radiographs per half day**.
- Expect interruptions (phone calls, Teams messages). They're intrusive, but you're helping people out — keep that in mind.
- These shifts are hard early in the year. They'll get easier, promise.
