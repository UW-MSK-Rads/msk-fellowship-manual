# Procedures at UH AM/PM

**Location:** G3/346

This rotation is where you build expertise across the full range of image-guided MSK interventions: spine injections (epidural, transforaminal, facet), vertebroplasties, biopsies, disc aspirations, joint injections and aspirations, CT- and US-guided soft tissue and bone biopsies, and image-guided tumor ablations. Fellows play a pivotal role in managing the procedures service.

## The basics

!!! tldr "If you read nothing else"
    - **Arrive:** by **7:15–7:30 AM** with all procedures pulled up on PACS, ready to run the list with attending by 7:30
    - **Primary role:** procedures + reviewing requests (green sheets)
    - **Secondary role:** dictate radiographs (target: 20+ per full day)
    - **Critical compliance rule:** faculty must be present from **needle-in to needle-out** — no exceptions

---

## The day before — case review checklist

Print the schedule from Health Link and make notes for each procedure. Discuss uncertain cases with faculty *now* — not the morning of.

For every case, review:

- [ ] **Procedure order** and any comments
- [ ] **Recent clinical note** from the ordering provider (sometimes the order doesn't reflect what they want — right vs. left, steroid vs. anesthetic only, correct level)
- [ ] **Previous imaging** (use this to plan your approach)
- [ ] **Previous procedure notes** if this is a repeat (look for tips on setup, needle gauge/length, patient anxiety)
- [ ] **Allergies, medications, comorbidities**
- [ ] **Transitional spinal anatomy and numbering**

!!! pro-tip "Pro tip"
    Make phone calls or e-communicate to resolve issues *in advance*. Problems that are hard to solve on procedure day are usually easy the day before.

## The morning timeline

=== "7:15 AM"
    **Arrive and set up.** Pull up all morning cases on PACS.

=== "7:30–7:45 AM"
    **Run the list with faculty.** Walk through every case.

=== "7:55 AM"
    **Tech brings patient back.** They'll get the patient from the main waiting room and bring them into the consenting/consult room. Be proactive to keep the day on time.

=== "Consent"
    Enter the consult room. Explain the procedure, answer questions, obtain written consent (iPad). Verify:

    - Allergies
    - Medications (especially blood thinners)
    - Not on antibiotics, no concurrent infection that contraindicates procedure

    Record pre-procedure pain score on the **pain reporting sheet**.

=== "Time-out"
    Escort patient into MP1. Perform a **TIMEOUT** with the tech and patient all in the same room. Tell the tech the position you want, then help them get the patient on the table.

=== "Setup"
    Set up the procedure (with faculty help early in the year, independently as the year progresses).

    !!! pro-tip "Practice the fluoro controls"
        The faster you get accustomed to the fluoro table controls, the easier every subsequent procedure becomes. Practice.

=== "Procedure"
    Perform the procedure. **Faculty must be in attendance from when the needle enters the skin until it is removed.** Critical for compliance, liability, and reimbursement.

    !!! warning "Spine injections — supervision required"
        Fellows are not credentialed to perform spine injections independently. Joint and US injections may be done unsupervised.

=== "Post"
    Help patient off table, verify they're doing okay. **Both you and the tech closely supervise** until clearly stable — vasovagal symptoms aren't uncommon, and some patients report leg weakness. Have a low threshold to wheelchair them out.

    Record post-procedure pain score and provide instructions on the pain reporting sheet.

=== "Cleanup + dictate"
    Clear all needles from the procedure tray. Tech takes patient back to waiting room.

    !!! danger "Dictate before the next case"
        Note your fluoro time and pre/post pain scores. **Dictate your procedure before starting the next case.** With templates this takes under a minute.

## Sedation cases

For procedures requiring conscious sedation (vertebroplasties, bone biopsies, ablations), there are extra steps:

??? abstract "Night before"
    - Place pre-procedure orders for admission to the **10-bed unit**
    - The MSK Nurse (Erica Timm, **263-6871**, pager **9585**) can help if needed

??? abstract "Morning of"
    - See the patient in the 10-bed pre/post area
    - Obtain consent, perform a focused H&P
    - Document the H&P in Health Link
    - Document a **separate attestation note** indicating you evaluated them prior to sedation
    - Someone can walk you through this the first time

??? abstract "After the procedure"
    - Patients return to 10-bed for recovery
    - Place post-procedure orders **immediately** after the procedure (order sets exist in HL)
    - For biopsies, place surgical pathology orders
    - **Always accompany the patient back to Recovery after any case with Anesthesiology** (fellow or faculty)

## Other responsibilities

??? note "Image confirmation and dictation"
    When dictating each case, it is **your responsibility** to assure that all images from the case have made it to PACS. Close the case as dictated when finished.

    Dictate every case you perform, after each case. Use the default template that pops up but add specific details if needed (different site for technical reasons, allergy accommodations, etc.).

??? note "Advanced procedure screening (green sheets)"
    You'll get a green sheet from a scheduler or the MSK CPC (Erica Timm) to evaluate a request for biopsy, vertebroplasty, or image-guided ablation.

    1. Research the case
    2. Discuss with faculty — is this one we'll do?
    3. **If not indicated:** communicate with the referring service. Explain why we're not doing it. Document a quick note in Health Link.
    4. **If approved:** return the sheet to the MSK CPC

??? note "Acrylic box in the Procedure RR"
    Faxed forms arrive for outside-provider patients scheduled for injections with us. They show up in the acrylic box on the wall.

    Your job: confirm the ordering info on the faxed sheets matches what's on the schedule. Write that on the sheet and return to Erica Timm. These cover all 3 sites (UH, EMH, SMC). Erica updates Health Link with notes confirming you reviewed it.

??? note "Inpatient consents"
    Occasionally you'll be asked to see an inpatient on the floor for consent for a biopsy, vertebroplasty, etc.

??? note "Vertebroplasty conference cases"
    Track your advanced procedures so you can present at the every-other-month vertebroplasty conference. Dr. Ross will email you to gather cases.

## Working with rotating residents

Mostly at the end of the year, a resident will rotate through procedures. The expectation:

1. Show them how to prepare and run the list for the day
2. Let them observe you consent a patient
3. Show them how to do a procedure
4. Gradually give them more responsibility
5. After the first few days, start alternating procedures (you get priority of which)
6. **Last week or two:** residents may fly solo, giving you more academic time
7. The resident will do **float** their last week of the procedure rotation for advanced procedure exposure
